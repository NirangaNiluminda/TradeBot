Is there a short-term edge in crypto prices?

Two years of 5-minute Binance candles for five coins, every question checked twice: once on older data where patterns are found, and again on newer data they have never seen.

Answer: no. Not one short-term pattern, entry hour or holding time made money after Binance's 0.2% round-trip fee in both periods, on any coin. The best ideas from the older data all lost money on the newer data. This matches the earlier strategy tests on 15-minute and 1-hour candles.
Finding 1 · the fee wall

Short moves are smaller than the fee

Every trade pays about 0.2% (0.1% to buy, 0.1% to sell). The bars show how far the price typically moves in each time window. Anything under the dashed line can't pay for the trade, even when you guess the direction right.

Finding 3 · time of day

No reliable "best hour", but a reliable busy hour

Which way the price moves in each hour isn't consistent: hours that looked good in the older data faded in the newer data. How much it moves is consistent: the US market open (18:30 to 21:30 Sri Lanka time) is clearly the busiest window on every coin.

Finding 4 · entry time × holding time

No entry hour and holding time beats the fee

For every combination of "buy at hour H" (24 options) and "sell K hours later" (1 to 12), we averaged the result after fees: 288 combinations per coin.

What this means

Next steps

Skip the short-term AI model (Step 2).A model learns from patterns in the data. With no pattern even close to the 0.2% fee, it would most likely learn noise and look good only on the data it was trained on.
Keep the 4-hour breakout bot running.Its trades aim for moves of 3 to 7%, where the fee is a small cost instead of a wall. It is still the only approach that held up on unseen data.
Possible later research:use an AI model as a filter that decides which breakouts to take, and test whether avoiding quiet hours improves breakout entries. Both would be tested the same way as here.
Data: Binance spot 5-minute candles (BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, XRP). Results are for buying (spot can't short) and include a 0.2% round-trip fee. "Older" = first 60% of the period, "newer" = last 40%. A pattern counts as confirmed only if it was profitable, beat a random entry, and had t ≥ 2 in both periods; on five simulated random coins this rule found zero false patterns.
